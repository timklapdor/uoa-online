The purpose of the visual design system was to enable a consistent and cohesive experience that could be developed by multiple contributors. The aim was to ensure the creayion of an engaging experience whilst maintaining the unique characteristics of each course, discipline, topics and authors. 

To achieve this the team developed a distinct visual language and common approaches for design decisions. Use of the design system meant teams weren't needing to reinvent or rethink the visual and graphic design by solving problems centrally. This allowed teams to spend more time on the learner experience, creating value-adds and clarity of purpose. 

This enabled the deliverable of each course, the MVP to switch from the traditional *Minimum Viable Product* to the **Maximum Value Product**. 

A sample of some of the unique components of the broader design system are outlined below.

## Cues and Directions

These two components provided learners with clear signposting throughout each course. 

### Cues

Cues visually communicated specific information about the content. These helped define for students critical types of content, rather than rely on their interpretation. This reduced the coginitive load on students to read AND interpret implied meaning of content. Cues helped direct learner attention to specific informationo on the page - to find definitions, see examples, be warned about sensitive content or to find the summary of specific topics or references. 

### Directions

Directions were developed to provide clear prompts for learners to act on. This helped to remove any ambiguity in regards what learners needed to do throughout the course. The directions also helped students recognise types of activity, allowing them to make choices in regards to how they would approach tasks and manage their time and effort.
