Description: >
  In this course, students explore the theory and practice of health promotion, its relationship with health service management and develop the skills essential for effective health promotion. Students explore the social determinants of health, models of behaviour change, and strategies for health promotion across various settings, including schools, indigenous communities, and occupational health and safety. The importance of community engagement, co-design, monitoring, and evaluation in health promotion planning and implementation is explored, and the role of health service managers in reorienting health systems towards prevention and enhancing population health and wellbeing is discussed. Through reflective practice and critical analysis, students develop a deep understanding of the complexities of health promotion and its impact on individuals and communities.

CLOs:
  - Evaluate and critique major approaches to health promotion (e.g. policy,
    behaviour change, community engagement, advocacy and social marketing).
  - Critically explain the historical, social and political context of major
    health promotion programs and theories, including the Ottawa Charter.
  - Apply major approaches to health promotion policy and theory to contemporary
    public health issues such as alcohol consumption, problem gambling and
    obesity.
  - Critically assess the applicability of major health promotion theories and
    strategies in vulnerable communities, including persons of low
    socio-economic status, Indigenous Australians, and refugees.
  - Identify and describe the key challenges facing health promotion programs in
    both developed and developing country context.

Assessments:
  - Name: Quiz
    Weighting: 15
    Type:
      - Multiple Choice Questions
    Description: In this task learners are tested on their knowledge about health promotion and disease prevention frameworks, health education and behaviour change theories, and factors that influence effective implementation of health promotion programs.
  - Name: Essay
    Weighting: 35
    Type:
      - Essay
    Description: Learners are asked to describe and critique major approaches to health promotion related to a specific condition for a particular population. They must also describe the evidence based approaches that could be implemented to address the health issue they have identified. Learners should include consideration of alignment with strategic health priorities, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander relevance and impact, equity issues, community partnership and engagement as well as communication and evaluation of the proposed program.
  - Name: Report
    Weighting: 35
    Type:
      - Report
    Description: For this task learners reflect on their health promotions proposal considering current policy and program approaches. This will include an  analysis of current challenges and potential new strategies to address them.
  - Name: Presentation
    Weighting: 15
    Type:
      - Media Task
    Description: "Learners are required to deliver a Pecha Kucha presentation, concise presentation format,  where they share their reflections, experiences, and understanding of the key concepts in the course. "
