One of the features of the OUA project was in positioning the role of the Learning Designer as central to the whole process. This wasn't to diminish other contributions, but rather to acknowledge the central role that Learning Designers play in supporting the course development process from beginning to end and facilitating collaboration with all of the indviduals involved. 

This page is aimed at describing the role that learning design and learning designers, played within the project. 

### What is Learning Design?
There are a lot of ways to think about design but ultimately design is a creative challenge. All creativity is problem-solving within a set of constraints. Design is one of these creative pursuits which seeks to solve a variety of problems through a set of available tools and different ways of communicating. So using this construct, learning design is setting the problem as learning, and then seeking to design a solution by understanding the motivations and experiences of the learners and instructors and then using the available media and medium to facilitate that learning.

> [!Callout]
> Learning design is a process, as opposed to a task.

It’s not an individual or singular thing, and you you can’t simply apply learning design to a course. Learning Design is the process of producing and crafting that course. Design isn't something that can be applied to a course, because it is the way of doing it.

The process of design includes developing an understanding of the audience, of the project constraints, the medium and the intent — all of which shape the final product. Design is the act of thinking about the whole, of negotiating and shaping the experience that the learner will have and the space in which it will happen.

## What does a Learning Designer do?
Describing exactly what a Learning Designer does in a day is a challenge. Being the instigator of a process means they are required to do a lot of varied tasks, but a lot of time is spent on relationships and collaboration that is required to happen between subject matter experts, graphic designers, media production, videographers, technologists and teachers in order for a course to be built.

Another thing that a Learning Designer does that is critical to the process is be an advocate for the learner. A lot of the design of the course, reviewing of material, development of media is based on placing the learner at the centre of the process and doing the work for them.

These aspects of the role - collaboration and advocating for the learner - drive a lot of the decision making and day-to-day tasks that a learning designer would do. From facilitating a workshop, reviewing content, planning a learning activity, to shooting a video - what they do is based on these two areas. They provide a way to connect staff and resources, they push for change in order for the learning experience to be better. The tasks themselves change over time, and as the course is developed what is required will change. That's the beauty of the role — it changes! It has many aspects and areas to utilise your skills and challenge yourself and develop new ones.

An outline of the tasks that are required to be completed is outlined in the Course Development Process.

## Roles & Responsibilities
In a collaborative relationship it can be difficult to manage the roles and responsibilities as there is often a lot of cross over and dialogue. Instead of specifics tasks it is more useful to think of the larger areas that learning designers contribtute:  

#### Alignment of outcomes with assessments and activities 
Ensure the relationship between these elements of the course is direct and explicit. You should ensure that each element of the course has a purpose and help to articulate that for the learner so that it becomes obvious.

#### Creatively solve problems 
A large component of developing an online course is interpreting, transitioning and transforming the teaching experience of a face-to-face class for delivery online. Sometimes that's as simple as changing the media - moving content from a lecture into a webpage or a video, but in some cases, it's transforming a simple and organic process from the classroom into one that works online and asynchronously – like turning to the person next to you and discussing your thoughts - how to you do that online?

#### Creating an active learning experience
Ensure that the learner is involved in their own learning by developing activities for them to engage with. Learning doesn't happen from osmosis, simply being adjacent to content doesn't mean you engage with it or learn from it. Ensure your course author is utilising all of the activity and learning types – and if not, work with them to find opportunities to.
